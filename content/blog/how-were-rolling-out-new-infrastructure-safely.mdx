We are rolling out new infrastructure designed to make Inngest more reliable, performant, and scalable for the future.

Changes this fundamental need to be tested carefully. After multiple rounds of internal testing and benchmarking, we are introducing the new infrastructure to production gradually, beginning with small cohorts of accounts on the Hobby plan. We will monitor each stage before expanding the rollout.

## Why we start with Hobby accounts

Internal testing can validate correctness and simulate load, but it cannot reproduce every workload, traffic pattern, or interaction that occurs in production. A staged rollout lets us learn from real-world usage while limiting the number of accounts exposed to a change at any one time.

The Hobby plan is intended for individual developers, evaluations, and small projects getting started with durable execution, rather than business-critical production workloads. Starting with Hobby accounts reduces the potential blast radius while we validate the infrastructure under production conditions. It also gives us room to pause, adjust, and resolve issues before rolling changes out more broadly. We use the same gradual approach for major infrastructure migrations; you can read more about the engineering behind our current work in [our FoundationDB migration series](/blog/migration-to-foundationdb-part-1?ref=blog-how-were-rolling-out-new-infrastructure-safely).

This means some Hobby accounts may experience temporary degraded performance during a rollout. We strive to keep any impact to a minimum by rolling out to small cohorts, monitoring the results closely, and addressing degraded performance before continuing.

## Options for evaluations and production workloads

If you are evaluating Inngest on a Hobby account and need a more predictable environment, [contact our Sales team](/contact?ref=blog-how-were-rolling-out-new-infrastructure-safely). We can help move your evaluation to infrastructure with stronger performance isolation.

Upgrading to [any paid plan](/pricing?ref=blog-how-were-rolling-out-new-infrastructure-safely) automatically moves your account to infrastructure that is isolated from the Hobby-plan rollout pool. Paid-plan infrastructure offers stronger performance isolation and is not used as the first stage for these rollouts.

## How to get support

If you experience unexpected latency or degraded performance, there are several ways to get help:

- [File a support request](https://support.inngest.com/) for account-specific help. Include the affected environment, function or run IDs, and the approximate time the issue occurred so our team can investigate.
- [Join the Inngest Discord](/discord?ref=blog-how-were-rolling-out-new-infrastructure-safely) to ask questions and connect with our team and community.
- [Check the Inngest status page](https://status.inngest.com/) for active incidents and platform updates. You can also subscribe there for notifications.
- Enterprise customers can contact us through their dedicated Slack channel. [Learn more about Enterprise support](/pricing?ref=blog-how-were-rolling-out-new-infrastructure-safely).

## Shipping with confidence

Production rollouts are an essential part of operating a reliable platform. Some behavior can only be understood under the variety and scale of real workloads, but that does not lessen our responsibility to protect the people building on Inngest.

Gradual rollouts allow us to improve the platform with confidence, learn quickly, and contain unexpected impact. We will continue investing in safeguards, monitoring, and infrastructure isolation as we build a more reliable and scalable foundation for every Inngest account.
