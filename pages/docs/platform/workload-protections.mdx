# Workload protections

Inngest runs a shared, multi-tenant platform. When one workload's traffic threatens the health of that platform, the Inngest team can take action on that specific workload to contain the impact.

This page explains those actions, what you will see in the dashboard, and what to do.

<Callout variant="info">
These are actions Inngest takes, not settings you configure. You cannot enable or disable them yourself, and they do not correspond to anything in your function's code.
</Callout>

## Draining

**Draining stops new runs from starting while allowing existing runs to finish.**

Inngest drains a function when it needs that function to stop taking on additional work — for example, when its volume is saturating shared infrastructure and affecting other workloads. Draining lets the work already underway complete while the source of new work is cut off.

A drained function shows a **Draining** badge in the dashboard, and Inngest emails you when the drain is applied.

### What happens while a function is draining

- **New triggers do not start runs.** Events, cron triggers, and invokes do not start runs for the function.
- **Existing runs continue normally.** Runs created before draining began keep going, including queued and sleeping runs.
- **Existing runs are not cancelled or paused.**
- **For [batched functions](/docs/guides/batching?ref=docs-workload-protections), new events are not added to a batch.**

<Warning>
Triggers skipped while a function is draining are not replayed automatically when the drain is lifted. Only future triggers start runs again. See [recovering skipped triggers](#recovering-skipped-triggers) below.
</Warning>

### Draining does not clear on its own

Draining is a persistent operational state, not a temporary progress indicator. It stays in effect until Inngest disables it — including after the last active run has finished. A function with no runs left is still draining, and still skipping new triggers.

### If you pause a draining function

[Pausing](/docs/guides/pause-functions?ref=docs-workload-protections) is a control you have; draining is not. Pausing a draining function does not clear the drain — both states remain set, and each is disabled separately, by you and by Inngest respectively.

When both are set:

- **New runs stay blocked.** For normal scheduling, pausing takes precedence as the reported skip reason.
- **Runs that draining allowed to continue become suspended** by the pause, in line with the pause option you choose. If you select "Cancel immediately", those runs are cancelled instead.
- **Unpausing resumes suspended runs, but the function is still draining.**

<Warning>
Unpausing does not lift a drain. It resumes the runs your pause suspended, but new triggers are still skipped until Inngest disables draining.
</Warning>

Because pausing a draining function suspends work that would otherwise have completed, it usually works against what the drain is doing. If you are considering pausing a draining function, contact support first.

### What to do while your function is draining

Draining is not caused by a bug in your code, and no deploy or sync will clear it.

1. **Check your email.** You should have received a message from Inngest when the drain was applied, explaining why. That is the best starting point for your specific case.
2. **Check [status.inngest.com](https://status.inngest.com)** for an active incident. Draining is often one part of a broader response.
3. **Contact support** to ask what is needed to lift the drain and when to expect that. Replying to the email you received keeps the context together.
4. **Reduce the function's incoming volume** if you can. This does not lift the drain, but it limits how much work is skipped while the drain is in effect.

### Recovering skipped triggers

Work skipped during a drain is not replayed automatically. To process it once the drain is lifted, use [Replay](/docs/platform/replay?ref=docs-workload-protections):

1. Navigate to the function's dashboard.
2. Select the **Replay** option in the "All actions" menu.
3. Select a date window covering the drain and enable the **Skipped** status.

Your plan's history limit bounds how far back you can replay, so recover skipped work before it ages out. See the [Replay guide](/docs/platform/replay?ref=docs-workload-protections) for details.

## Why Inngest takes these actions

Run scheduling, batching, pause processing, and async step operations all route through shared, horizontally sharded infrastructure. A single workload producing an unusual volume or shape of work can saturate one shard, degrading every other workload on it.

Acting on the specific workload contains that blast radius. It is a last resort, used when scaling and rebalancing are not enough or not fast enough. The [April 15, 2026 incident report](/blog/2026-04-17-incident-report-for-april-15-2026?ref=docs-workload-protections) walks through a case where draining was used this way.

## Limits you control

Workload protections are distinct from the limits that apply to your account and the flow control you configure yourself:

- **[Usage limits](/docs/usage-limits/inngest?ref=docs-workload-protections)** are the plan and platform limits that apply to every account.
- **[Concurrency](/docs/guides/concurrency?ref=docs-workload-protections)**, **[throttling](/docs/guides/throttling?ref=docs-workload-protections)**, and **[rate limiting](/docs/guides/rate-limiting?ref=docs-workload-protections)** shape how quickly your runs start.
- **[Pausing](/docs/guides/pause-functions?ref=docs-workload-protections)** stops new runs and suspends runs in progress.

Staying well inside these limits, and using flow control to smooth bursty workloads, is the most reliable way to avoid needing a protection in the first place.
